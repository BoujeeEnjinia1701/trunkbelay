# TrunkBelay user requirements

What the people in a palm rescue need, written from their side. The product requirements that meet these needs are in TKB-PRS-001 (`03-requirements.md`) and the links are in the traceability matrix TKB-RTM-001 (`03b-traceability.md`). The needs are drawn from the reported rescues and the users and stakeholders in TKB-PRB-001; none has yet been confirmed with a climber, a fire crew or a trainer, so all are proposals for co-design. Method: STANDARDS section 22.

> **Safety:** These needs describe rescue equipment that holds a person at height. Meeting them on paper does not make the kit safe; each is confirmed by test at TRL 4.

Table 1. User requirements.

| ID | Category | Title | Need | Priority | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UR-001 | Safety | Holds on a wet palm | The anchor holds the stranded person on a wet, fibrous, often curved palm trunk without slipping | High | Rescuer; stranded climber (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-002 | Safety | Holds as the load moves | The anchor stays secure while the person's weight comes onto it and as they are lowered past it | High | Rescuer (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-003 | Deployment and assembly | Fits the palms we climb | One kit fits the coconut palms climbed locally, about 200 to 450 mm across, with no tools | High | Rescuer; climbers' collective (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-004 | Usability | Fast at height | Once beside the victim, the rescuer can fix the anchor and take the victim's weight within a few minutes | High | Stranded climber; rescuer (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-005 | Performance | Controlled lowering | One rescuer can lower the person smoothly, at walking pace or slower, without a strong grip | High | Rescuer (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-006 | Performance | Reaches the ground | The rope reaches the ground from the tallest palms climbed, about 25 m | High | Rescuer; helpers on the ground (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-007 | Logistics | Light to carry up | What the rescuer carries up the trunk is light and hangs from the harness, leaving both hands free to climb | High | Rescuer (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-008 | Environment and durability | No harm to the palm | Using the kit does not cut or wound the palm | Medium | Palm owners and farmers (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-009 | Cost | Affordable to a group | A climber group or a training scheme can afford a kit | Medium | Trainer; Coconut Development Board (PRB Tables 2 and 3) |
| UR-010 | Usability | Learnable in half a day | Climbers new to the kit can run a supervised rescue drill after a half-day session | Medium | Trainer; fire crew (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-011 | Safety | Holds if the rescuer lets go | If the rescuer is hit, faints or lets go, the person is still held | High | Stranded climber; rescuer (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-012 | Safety | Head up once freed | Once freed from the climbing device, the person hangs head up and cannot slip out | High | Stranded climber (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-013 | Environment and durability | Survives the climate | The kit survives heat, humidity, monsoon rain and storage in a shed | Medium | Climbers (PRB operating environment) |
| UR-014 | Maintenance | Easy to inspect and renew | Parts that wear can be checked by eye and replaced locally | Low | Trainer; climbers' collective (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-015 | Regulatory | Fits rescue practice | The kit uses rescue gear that fire crews recognise and is never presented as certified equipment | Medium | Fire and rescue crew (PRB Table 2) |
| UR-016 | Deployment and assembly | Made and mended locally | A local workshop can make and repair the made parts | Medium | Local fabricators; trainer (PRB Table 3) |
| UR-017 | Regulatory | Free to build and share | Anyone can build, adapt and share the design without infringing others' patents | Medium | Design Molecule; co-design partners (PRB Table 3) |

Categories: deployment and assembly, performance, usability, safety, environment and durability, logistics, maintenance, cost, regulatory. Priorities: High, Medium, Low.
